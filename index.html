The filter

A game qualifies when the model’s number crosses exactly one key NFL number (0, 3, 6, 7, 10) away from the market’s, and the play is a road team inside 6 points or a home team beyond 6. Crossing several keys at once means the model has drifted far from the market, and historically that is when it is least accurate — not most.

How these were tested, and what the test actually said

Every number here comes from a random-forest spread model built on prior-season player grades. Because the shipped model was trained on every completed season, it was retrained for each test season using only earlier data, then graded blind against closing lines — 1,846 games, 2019 to 2025.

Bet everything the model says and you lose. Straight up it went 51.8% against a 52.4% break-even, and 48.7% across 2023–25. Its average error was 10.75 points against the market’s 9.83, so the market is the better forecaster on most games. That result is why this page is a short list rather than a full card.

What I do not claim. The filter was found by searching one dataset, so its significance is weaker than the headline suggests — the honest support is that it held up in 6 of 7 seasons without decaying, not the p-value. Roughly 41 bets a season means small samples and losing weeks are normal. No graded forward results exist yet; every one of these is being tracked and will be published win or lose.

Injuries come from roster designations only — the weekly injury report is not published for 2026 yet, so short-term absences are not reflected. Weather is not modeled.
