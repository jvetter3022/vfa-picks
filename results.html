The selection rule changed after week 1

Week 1’s five plays were chosen by an automatic filter — exactly one key number crossed, plus a side-and-edge profile that held up in backtest. From week 2 the card has been chosen by hand off the same model’s slate instead.

That matters for reading the week-by-week table above: the difference between week 1 and week 2 is not evidence about the model, because the way plays were picked changed at the same time. Both are included because both were actually published; ten bets across two different selection rules is not a track record for either.

What this record is not

These are the plays backed off one model, priced against one book’s line at the time. The underlying model does not beat the closing line across the board: measured over 1,846 historical games it ran a mean absolute error of 10.885 against the market’s 9.826, hit 51.5%, and returned −1.69%. The plays above are a filtered subset, and a filtered subset of a losing series is not automatically a winning one.

The 48–34–1 figure from 2025 that appears elsewhere came from a different selection rule and is not part of this series, so it is deliberately not pooled into the numbers above.
